# Speaker Script — Two Neighborhoods, Two Communications Strategies

**Total runtime: ~8–10 minutes** (slow, deliberate pace) · 9 slides

A working script. Read it aloud, mark it up, change anything that doesn't sound like you. The cadence in brackets `[pause]` matters more than the exact words.

## Slide 1 — Title (0:00 – 0:20)

> Hi, I'm [your name]. Over the next ten minutes I want to walk you through how I'd approach communications for two neighborhoods in your service area: **The Dunes** and **Palm Haven**. [pause] I'll show you the data, point out the one insight that matters most, and then lay out a concrete plan for the first ninety days. [pause] Let's get into it.

**Coaching note:** Land the names slowly. They'll come up nine more times.

---

## Slide 2 — The setup (0:20 – 0:50)

> Two neighborhoods, both inside the same service area. The Dunes is larger — twelve hundred residents. Palm Haven is smaller — eight hundred. [pause] From an org chart they're equivalent line items. [pause] From a communications standpoint, they could not be more different. [pause] Every chart you're about to see is a reason to stop sending the *same* flyer to *both*.

**Coaching note:** The "same flyer to both" line is the thesis. Slow down on it.

---

## Slide 3 — Slope chart, the overview (0:50 – 1:30)

> Before we dive in, here's the whole picture. Five metrics, both neighborhoods, one image. [pause] Each line is a metric. The steeper the line, the bigger the gap. [pause] Notice the BMI line and the income-under-twenty-five-K line — those are the two steepest. [point if in person] [pause] And the income-over-forty-K line goes the other way: Palm Haven is way ahead. [pause] Now let me zoom in on what matters most.

**Coaching note:** Resist the urge to read every number. Let the eye see "steep = bad gap."

---

## Slide 4 — BMI, the headline (1:30 – 2:30)

> This is the single most important number in the deck. [pause] **Fifty-nine percent** of Dunes residents have elevated BMI. **Twenty-three percent** in Palm Haven. [pause] That's a thirty-six-point gap. [long pause] For context: a thirty-six-point gap is the difference between a public-health emergency and a healthy community. [pause] So if I'm running comms for both neighborhoods, health messaging in Palm Haven is one campaign a year. [pause] In The Dunes, it's the through-line of everything we publish.

**Coaching note:** This is the emotional center of the talk. Don't rush. Hold eye contact. The "long pause" after 36-point gap is your most important silence.

---

## Slide 5 — Income at opposite ends (2:30 – 3:20)

> Income flips the script. [pause] Sixty-nine percent of Dunes residents earn under twenty-five thousand a year. [pause] In Palm Haven, forty-five percent earn over forty thousand. [pause] The same channel mix doesn't reach both. [pause] Print and SMS over data-heavy email. Free and in-person over premium and digital. [pause] And critically — language and reading level need to match. We'll come back to that.

**Coaching note:** "Flips the script" cues the audience that the chart's direction reverses. They'll appreciate the heads-up.

---

## Slide 6 — All metrics together (3:20 – 4:10)

> One last view of the data — all five metrics together as a lollipop. [pause] Notice the three things you can read in two seconds: [pause] One — The Dunes skews younger. Thirty-one percent under eighteen versus sixteen. [pause] Two — fifteen percent of Dunes households use Spanish as their primary language. Two and a half times Palm Haven. [pause] Three — that income flip we just talked about. [pause] Younger, lower-income, more linguistically diverse. [pause] That's not a profile you serve with a single voice.

**Coaching note:** "Three things in two seconds" is an honest claim about good viz. Use it.

---

## Slide 7 — Two playbooks (4:10 – 5:30)

> So what does this mean for me as your Communications Director? [pause] Two playbooks, not one. [pause]
>
> For The Dunes — **bilingual by default**. Spanish-first content, not translated-as-an-afterthought. [pause] Health is the through-line. [pause] Trusted messengers — schools, faith leaders, promotoras de salud — do the heavy lifting. [pause] Print, SMS, in-person. Not email-first.
>
> For Palm Haven — email and digital are fine. [pause] Lead with civic engagement, advocacy, volunteering. [pause] Self-serve resources work — they have the bandwidth and the time. [pause] Monthly newsletter, longer form, opt-in.

**Coaching note:** This is the "so what" slide. Let it breathe. If you only have time for one thing, this is it.

---

## Slide 8 — First 90 days (5:30 – 7:00)

> And here's how I'd sequence it. [pause]
>
> First thirty days — **audit**. I don't translate anything, change anything, or launch anything until I understand what's already in market. [pause]
>
> Next thirty — **build the bilingual workflow**. Hire or contract a Spanish-language editor. Build the calendar. [pause]
>
> Final thirty — **re-mix the channels**. SMS for The Dunes. A clean email program for Palm Haven. Sunset what isn't working. [pause]
>
> By day ninety, the goal is simple — **no resident gets the wrong message in the wrong language on the wrong channel.**

**Coaching note:** The closing line should land like a thesis. It is one. Memorize it.

---

## Slide 9 — Q&A (7:00 – 7:30)

> Thank you. I'd love to take questions. [pause] A few I'd anticipate: How do I prioritize when budgets are tight? What metrics would I report on? How do I build trust in a community that has been talked *at* instead of *with*? [pause] Happy to go anywhere.

**Coaching note:** Inviting your own hard questions signals confidence. If you're nervous about Q&A, this works.

---

## Anticipated questions & one-paragraph answers

**"What's the first thing you'd ship?"**
A bilingual SMS pilot in The Dunes. SMS hits a 98% open rate, doesn't require data, and a pilot proves out the bilingual workflow before we scale it everywhere. Cheap, fast, measurable.

**"How do you measure success?"**
Reach by neighborhood (not aggregate), engagement by channel, and outcome metrics tied to the health and civic goals — clinic visits, program enrollments, town hall attendance. Aggregate dashboards hide the gap; segmented dashboards make sure we keep closing it.

**"What's the budget assumption?"**
The 90-day plan is mostly a re-allocation, not a net add. SMS and a Spanish editor are real costs. They're funded by sunsetting the channels the audit shows aren't working.

**"How do you build trust in The Dunes?"**
Show up before you ask. Partner with promotoras and the schools. Spanish-first, not Spanish-translated. And measure trust — pre/post surveys at every event — so we know if we're earning it.
